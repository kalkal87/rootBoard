# Findings: Five Whys

## Framework
- **Name:** Five Whys
- **Slug:** five-whys
- **Lens summary:** Traces a stated symptom back through repeated "why"
  questions to find the structural root cause underneath it.

## Observations
- Signups have been flat, "maybe down slightly," for about 6 weeks
  (founder note, line 1).
- Twitter ads cost-per-click "crept up," though the founder isn't sure by
  how much (founder note, line 3).

## Assumptions
- Assuming flat signups and rising CPC are related — the input places them
  in the same note, but never says the founder actually checked whether
  the two move together.

## Open Questions
- Did signups drop before or after CPC rose, or are these two unrelated
  observations the founder is pattern-matching together?
- What does "maybe down slightly" mean in actual numbers? The input never
  gives a figure.

## Problems Identified

### Problem 1: Rising acquisition cost may be suppressing signup volume
- **Problem Statement:** Signups may be flattening because the primary
  paid channel (Twitter ads) is getting more expensive per click — which,
  if true, would mean fewer clicks bought for the same spend. The chain
  stops here because the input doesn't establish that the two are actually
  linked, only that both were noticed around the same time.
- **Impact / Symptoms:** Flat-to-slightly-down signups for about 6 weeks;
  the founder flagged the channel cost and the signup dip in the same
  breath.
- **Proof Points:**
  - "New signups have been flat for like 6 weeks now, maybe down
    slightly." (founder note, line 1)
  - "Our biggest channel (Twitter ads) engagement felt lower lately, cost
    per click crept up according to the dashboard, not sure by how much
    though." (founder note, line 3)
- **Confidence:** Low

## Coverage Note
Five Whys only got one real "why" deep before the input ran out of
support — the founder hasn't investigated yet, by their own admission. This
is a thin-input case: the chain above is the most likely path visible in
the notes, not a confirmed root cause, and it should be read that way.
